    What a weekly sample misses during snowmelt

    This is a starter post, written so you can see how the section looks with real content. Replace the text with your own and the formatting carries over.

    The argument is simple enough to state in a sentence: if the event lasts two to five days and you sample every seven, you will usually miss it. What is less obvious is what that does to a load estimate. Interpolating between two samples that both sit on baseflow produces a smooth, plausible-looking curve that happens to be wrong by a margin nobody can see from the record itself.

    A useful exercise is to take a sub-daily record and subsample it at weekly and monthly intervals, then recompute the load from each. The spread across different starting days is often larger than the difference between catchments, which is an uncomfortable result if catchment comparison is the point of the monitoring programme.

    Replace this with a quotation, a caveat, or a result you want to pull out of the running text.

    Where the evidence is thin, say so. I would not claim yet that the pattern holds across regimes, only that it shows up clearly in the melt data I have.
